Overview: A guillotine fantasy football league is a survival-style fantasy football format where the lowest-scoring team is eliminated each week, and their entire roster is sent to the waiver wire for the remaining managers to pick up. There are no head-to-head matchups; your only goal every week is to avoid having the absolute lowest score. Core Rules and Mechanics: No Matchups: Standings are based purely on total weekly points, not a win-loss record. Weekly Elimination: The lowest-scoring team is "chopped" from the league, ending their season. The Loot Pool: When a team is eliminated, all of their star players go directly to the waiver wire. Bidding Wars: Surviving managers use a FAAB (Free Agent Acquisition Budget) system to bid on the newly available elite players. The Finale: The weekly chopping continues until only two teams remain to fight for the championship in the final weeks of the NFL season. Cost: $5 per team. Winner takes all Number of Teams: Depends on how many want to play. 18 teams maximum. Scoring: We use the same scoring rules as the Mag 7 and Cousin FFLs Players per team: Each team will have 12 players. 8 starters and 4 bench players: 1 QB 3 RB 3 WR 2 TE 3 Flex Each team must keep this exact number of players at each position for the entire season. The three flex can be any position. This prevents a team from hoarding superstar players and then not starting them. No kickers or defense in the league. Starters per week: 1 QB 2 RB 2 WR 1 TE 1 Flex Free Agent Acquisition Budget: In a guillotine fantasy football league, FAAB (Free Agent Acquisition Budget) functions as a blind-auction pool of fake money used to buy players from teams that get eliminated every week. Each team has a budget of $1,000 to use throughout the season to bid on players. ○ The Chopping Block: Each week, the lowest-scoring team is eliminated from the league. ○ Releasing Players: All players from the chopped team go onto the waiver wire as free agents. ○ Blind Bidding: Managers submit private, secret dollar bids using their remaining FAAB budget to claim these players. The highest bidder wins the player when waivers process. Core Rules and Dynamics ○ Starting Budget: Every manager begins the year with the same total amount ($1,000). ○ Minimum Bids: You can bid as low as $0 on a player. ○ Ties: If two managers bid the exact same amount, league tie-breaker settings decide who gets the player. The tie breaker is total points, team with the lowest total points gets the player. If there is still a tie then the team with the lowest scoring starter from the previous week gets the player. If there is still a tie then the tiebreaker moves up to comparing the lowest scoring second starter, third starter, etc until the tie is broken. A couple points to consider regarding FAAB Since our leagues do not use FAAB it is up to each owner to determine their strategy. However, here are a couple of things to consider… ○ Accelerating Value: Early in the season, there are many teams and lots of total money floating around, making individual dollars less scarce. Later in the year, remaining teams have less money left, making every single dollar much more powerful. ○ The Survival Balance: Spend too much early, and you won't have cash to buy elite players when only a few stacked teams are left. Save too much, and your weak roster might get "chopped" before you ever spend it. Draft: This is always the toughest part…getting on everyone's schedule due to work, life, etc. There are two options and this document will be updated with the option that seems best for most of the players: 1. No draft. Use the rosters that owners already have drafted in the Mag 7 or Cousins league. If an owner has a team in both leagues they must choose which roster to use. Note that whichever roster that owner chooses is the one used for the entirety of the guillotine league. With not draft there will be players on more than one team. We can make that work but note that the ESPN app does not support more than one team having the same player. So we may need to use a different app (one possibility is called MyFantasyLeague since that app allows more than one team to have the same player). One other detail is that the same team cannot have the same player twice. This could happen if the same player is on two teams and one of the teams gets 'chopped'. The surviving owner with that player cannot pick up that player. For example, Team A and Team B both have Bijon Robinson. Team B has the lowest score that week and gets chopped. All of Team B's players are added to the waiver pool. Team A cannot put in a claim for Bijon Robinson since they already have him on their roster. 2. We have a draft. ESPN allows automatic drafting if a player cannot attend or can only attend part of the draft. If time is really tight we can have a shortened draft. In this case the draft would last 8 rounds and each team would draft their starting lineup. Then each week teams can add to their roster one player until a full team of 12 players is formed. Prior to week one, if a team has a player that is hurt or cannot play they can add a free agent to their team and not have to drop a player (in this case the team would have 9 players instead of 8 for week one).
